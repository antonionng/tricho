# Course review checklist

Karley Weir Ghallagher is named as the reviewer on all three courses, so her name appears on every course page and certificate. Please read each course in full before it goes live, and tick off the points below. They are the places where the writing depends on a fact, a figure or a legal statement that should be confirmed.

The lessons are in `src/content/course-lessons/`. The easiest way to read them is as a learner: go to **Studio > Courses** and choose **Read the course as a learner**. Team members can open any course without enrolling; answers and notes aren't saved while you preview.

If something needs changing, note the lesson and the wording, and the change can be made in the lesson file.

## The scalp consultation for stylists and head spa therapists

`scalp-consultation-for-stylists`, six lessons, 15-question assessment.

- [ ] Shedding timings: shedding starts about 2 to 3 months after a trigger; normal loss is about 50 to 100 hairs a day; shedding after childbirth starts about 2 to 4 months after the birth, with a GP review if it lasts more than about 6 months.
- [ ] Kerion and tinea capitis: the description (boggy swelling, pustules, swollen glands, "can be mistaken for an abscess") and the "same or next day GP or NHS 111" urgency.
- [ ] Warning signs of skin cancer on the scalp (BCC, SCC, melanoma) in Red flags and Aftercare conversations.
- [ ] Descriptions of scarring hair loss: FFA "most often in women after the menopause" and CCCA "most often in women of African descent".
- [ ] The urgency table in Red flags: which signs count as an emergency, same or next day, within a week or two, or routine.
- [ ] Health information in consultation records and photos treated as special category data under UK GDPR and the Irish Data Protection Acts.
- [ ] Parent or guardian consent for under-16s (the course also tells learners to follow their insurer's rules).
- [ ] "Cosmetic products cannot lawfully be marketed as treating hair loss."
- [ ] Trichology described as "not statutorily regulated in the UK or Ireland".
- [ ] Minoxidil described as a licensed medicine available over the counter from pharmacies in the UK and Ireland, with no dosing advice. Confirm this matches current availability in Ireland.
- [ ] High-dose biotin can affect some blood test results (Aftercare conversations).
- [ ] Safeguarding contacts: local authority social care, Tusla in Ireland, the NSPCC helpline, 999 or 112, and Samaritans on 116 123 for the UK and Ireland.
- [ ] Ludwig, Sinclair and Norwood scales named as tools clinicians use, with learners told to describe thinning in plain words instead.
- [ ] The referral note template uses "NVQ Level 3" as an example qualification. Change it if you'd prefer one that applies in both the UK and Ireland.
- [ ] The cases involving young people (Kofi, 7; Tyrone, 8; Sophie, 12; Leah, 14, possible hair pulling) are handled sensitively.

## Japanese head spa: foundations and safe practice

`japanese-head-spa-foundations`, eight lessons, 20-question assessment.

- [ ] The 2016 study (Koyama et al., Eplasty): 9 healthy men, device massage for 4 minutes a day for 24 weeks, thicker hairs but no significant change in hair count. The follow-up survey and the small office-worker stress studies are described without citations.
- [ ] History: anma, massage and shiatsu described as regulated professions in Japan, given as one reason salon head spa is framed as scalp care. The rest of the history is deliberately hedged.
- [ ] Contraindication lists, including the pregnancy guidance (caution in the first trimester, positioning on the back).
- [ ] Essential oils: epilepsy-related oils (rosemary, sage, fennel, camphor-rich oils); dilution of about 1% (1 drop per 5ml) for scalp and face and rarely above 2%; bergamot and expressed lime named as photosensitising. Check against your insurer's terms and a recognised aromatherapy reference.
- [ ] No fixed waiting periods are given after a hair transplant or a chemical service; learners are told to follow the surgeon or the manufacturer. Confirm you're happy with that.
- [ ] Legionella figures, attributed to HSE ACoP L8 and HSG274: growth at 20 to 45°C, hot water stored at 60°C or above and reaching outlets at 50°C or above within about a minute, cold water below 20°C, weekly flushing, spray heads cleaned at least every three months. The Northern Ireland and Republic of Ireland references are general.
- [ ] London special treatment licence, cited under the London Local Authorities Act 1991.
- [ ] UK VAT threshold given as £90,000 (Irish thresholds aren't stated).
- [ ] UK consumer law (the DMCC Act) now specifically prohibits fake reviews.
- [ ] Most UK businesses that hold client records electronically need to pay the ICO data protection fee.
- [ ] The CAP Code and ASAI Code are referred to in general terms, without rule numbers.
- [ ] Beauty parlour stroke syndrome: described as rare and first reported in the early 1990s, with 999 (UK) and 112 or 999 (Ireland).
- [ ] The pricing worked example is illustrative (£16 an hour staff cost, £18 per room hour, £4,850 of equipment over 1,380 treatments, giving a cost of £62.81 and menu prices of £88, or £105 with VAT).
- [ ] The practical assessment assumes the learner arranges an experienced practitioner to observe them, or records a treatment with the model's consent and reviews it themselves. Change this if Trichollective will run the assessment differently.
- [ ] Rebooking intervals (2 to 4, 4, and 6 to 8 weeks) are presented as suggestions based on the client's goal, not as evidence-based.

## Working across disciplines: referrals that help the client

`working-across-disciplines`, four lessons, 12-question assessment.

- [ ] Regulation: the Hair and Barber Council's voluntary register in the UK; no statutory regulator for hairdressers or trichologists in Ireland; the regulator names (GPhC, PSNI, PSI, NMC, NMBI, GMC, Medical Council); only English transplant clinics register with the Care Quality Commission (the other nations and Ireland aren't covered).
- [ ] Routes: NHS 111 in England and Wales, NHS 24 on 111 in Scotland, GP out-of-hours in Northern Ireland and Ireland; the Northern Ireland Health and Social Care Trust Gateway team for children; HSE safeguarding and protection teams for adults at risk in Ireland; the NSPCC helpline (named, no number); NHS Talking Therapies self-referral (England only); Samaritans on 116 123.
- [ ] The cancer pathway is called the "urgent suspected cancer referral, previously the two-week wait in England", with no target timescales. Confirm the wording for Ireland, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- [ ] Giant cell arteritis red flags: over about 50, scalp tenderness, jaw pain on chewing, vision change; same day, or an emergency if vision is lost.
- [ ] Anaphylaxis first aid wording, including helping a client use their own adrenaline auto-injector.
- [ ] "Urgent" for a painful blistering rash near the eye and for a boggy swelling on a child's scalp.
- [ ] Postpartum shedding reassurance (no precise timescales are given).
- [ ] Signs of scarring hair loss and frontal fibrosing alopecia.
- [ ] Data protection: explicit consent advised as the practical basis for independent practitioners sharing health information, with regulated professionals pointed to their own regulators. Confirm for UK GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018 (UK) and the GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018 (Ireland), and check the guidance on handling a data breach.
- [ ] Children First Act 2015 (Ireland): registered health professionals described as mandated persons; hairdressers aren't named.
- [ ] Trichologists can arrange private blood tests, but the GP acts on abnormal results.
- [ ] Pharmacists supply topical minoxidil; finasteride is prescription-only.
- [ ] Clients may choose to see a private dermatologist and take the letter themselves. The course never says a non-doctor can refer directly into NHS or HSE hospital care.
